Why most estimates die — and how to fix it in 3 touches

82% of contractors never follow up after sending an estimate. That single gap costs the average crew over $2,800/month in lost revenue. The client didn't say no — they got busy, distracted, or started getting quotes elsewhere. This sequence keeps you in front of them without being pushy. Use it verbatim or adapt it. What matters is that you do it consistently, on schedule, every time.

The Rules That Make This Work

Send Day 1 within 2 hours of the estimate. Schedule Day 3 and Day 7 reminders the same day you send the estimate — before you forget. Use the actual copy above; it's tested. Adapt the merge fields [IN BRACKETS] to your specific job.

Stop Doing These Things

Don't say "just following up." Don't send three emails in a row without calling. Don't apologize for reaching out. Don't give up after one touch. And don't forget to log responses — the tracker above is your system.

After Day 7 — What Comes Next

If no response: set a 30-day reminder to re-engage. If they say "not yet": ask when to circle back and put it in your calendar. If they say no: ask why — that data makes your next estimate better.

When They Say Yes — Move Fast

When a lead responds positively, respond within 2 hours with next steps. Confirm start date, deposit, and what they need to provide. Speed at conversion is the difference between a signed deal and "we went with someone else."
